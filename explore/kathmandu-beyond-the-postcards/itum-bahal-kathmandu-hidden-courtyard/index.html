Itum Bahal: The Buddhist Courtyard Hidden in Kathmandu

Itum Bahal is one of the largest Buddhist bahals in Kathmandu and appears on the Nepal Tourism Board's Kathmandu Heritage Walk. Its courtyards, shrines and carved details reward a slower walk away from the main streets.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · FIELD GUIDE

The story behind the place

Start with the Nepal Tourism Board's heritage-walk description, which identifies Itum Bahal among Kathmandu's key heritage stops. A bahal is a traditional Buddhist courtyard monastery complex: the courtyard is not simply an attractive shortcut, but a living religious and community space. Look carefully at the arrangement of shrines, entrances and surrounding lanes rather than treating the site as a photo backdrop.

How to experience it

The best way to experience Itum Bahal is on foot as part of an old-city heritage walk. Keep voices low, avoid blocking doorways, ask before photographing people or rituals, and follow any signs or instructions. Opening arrangements can vary because this is a living heritage setting; confirm locally before making a special trip.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

Is Itum Bahal worth visiting?

Yes, especially for travellers interested in Kathmandu's Newar Buddhist heritage and courtyard architecture. It is best combined with other old-city bahals and lanes on a walking route.
