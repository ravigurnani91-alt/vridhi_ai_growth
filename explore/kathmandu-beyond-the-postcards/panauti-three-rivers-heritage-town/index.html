Panauti: The Nepalese Town Built Around Three Sacred Rivers

Panauti is a historic town in the Kathmandu Valley region, known for its temples, traditional settlement and sacred river landscape. Nepal's Department of Archaeology includes its early medieval architectural complex on the Tentative List.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · FIELD GUIDE

The story behind the place

Panauti's identity is shaped by the meeting of rivers and by a dense concentration of religious and domestic architecture. The sacred confluence is central to local belief and pilgrimage. The town's tentative-list status should be stated accurately: it is recognised on Nepal's Tentative List, not currently inscribed as a standalone UNESCO World Heritage property.

How to experience it

Panauti can be visited as a day trip from Kathmandu, but allow time to walk the old lanes and riverfront rather than rushing between monuments. Confirm transport, local events and access before travelling. Support local shops and food businesses when possible.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

Is Panauti a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Its early medieval architectural complex is on Nepal's World Heritage Tentative List, not the main World Heritage List.
