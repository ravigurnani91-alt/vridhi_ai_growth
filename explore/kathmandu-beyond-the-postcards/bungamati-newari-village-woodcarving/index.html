Bungamati: The Newar Village Where Craft Is Part of Daily Life

Bungamati is a traditional Newar settlement south of central Kathmandu, known for its heritage streets, craft traditions and religious life.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · FIELD GUIDE

The story behind the place

The village is best understood as a living community rather than an open-air museum. Its woodcarving workshops and traditional architecture are part of local livelihoods and identity. The Rato Machhindranath tradition is shared across the wider valley and has its own complex ritual calendar; confirm current festival routes and dates with local organisers.

How to experience it

Visit during daylight, buy directly from makers where possible, and ask before photographing workshops or homes. Some heritage structures and neighbourhoods have been affected by earthquake recovery and rebuilding; check current site conditions and respect private or restricted spaces.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

What is Bungamati known for?

It is known for Newar heritage, traditional architecture, woodcarving and its connection with the Rato Machhindranath tradition.
