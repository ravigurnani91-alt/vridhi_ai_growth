KATHMANDU, BEYOND THE POSTCARDS · FIELD GUIDE

The Naropa and Tilopa Cave Traditions Near Pashupatinath

The Pashupatinath area contains a dense sacred landscape along the Bagmati River. Some smaller cave and shrine sites are associated in Buddhist tradition with the mahasiddhas Naropa and Tilopa.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · 4 MIN READ

The story behind the place

The names Naropa and Tilopa belong to influential figures in Buddhist history and lineage traditions. Local sites associated with them should be described with care: an association in religious tradition does not by itself establish the exact date or historical identity of a cave. The wider Pashupatinath precinct is a major Hindu sacred complex and a UNESCO-inscribed monument zone within the Kathmandu Valley property.

How to experience it

Treat the area as an active religious landscape. Check which spaces are open to visitors, follow local restrictions and dress respectfully. Do not assume every cave or shrine is publicly accessible, and avoid entering secluded spaces without local guidance.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

Are the Naropa and Tilopa caves archaeologically dated?

Publicly accessible authoritative dating evidence is limited; their significance is primarily presented through Buddhist religious and local traditions.

Sources & further reading

This guide draws on UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Religious traditions, local accounts and documented historical evidence are identified separately; claims not supported by the cited material are not presented as established fact.
