Nagi Gumba: A Forest Monastery Above Kathmandu

Nagi Gumba is commonly visited as part of the forested hills above Budhanilkantha and the Shivapuri area. This guide focuses on the experience and practical checks rather than making unsupported claims about the monastery's age or history.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · FIELD GUIDE

The story behind the place

The appeal is the contrast: a forested hillside close to a busy capital, with a Buddhist nunnery setting and walking routes in the surrounding hills. Treat the monastery as a place of practice, not an attraction staged for visitors. Ask locally whether visitors are welcome on the day and which paths are open.

How to experience it

Check current Shivapuri access rules, any required entry permits or fees, weather and trail conditions before leaving. Wear proper walking shoes, carry water, avoid loud music and do not photograph residents or religious activity without permission. This page does not claim live trail or opening status.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

Can you visit Nagi Gumba?

Visitors commonly include it in walks above Budhanilkantha, but access and visitor etiquette should be confirmed locally before setting out.
