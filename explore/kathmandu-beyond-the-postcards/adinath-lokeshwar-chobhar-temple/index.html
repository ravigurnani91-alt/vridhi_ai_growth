KATHMANDU, BEYOND THE POSTCARDS · FIELD GUIDE

Why Is Chobhar's Adinath Temple Covered in Pots and Plates?

Above Chobhar, the Adinath Lokeshwar shrine is a distinctive stop on the southern edge of the Kathmandu Valley. The Nepal Tourism Board's regional material describes the hilltop shrine and its panoramic setting.

VRIDHI EDITORIAL TEAM · 23 NOVEMBER 2026 · 4 MIN READ

The story behind the place

The temple's metal utensils and household objects make it visually unusual. Rather than reducing them to decoration, look at them as devotional offerings connected to everyday life. The site also sits within a landscape shaped by the Bagmati gorge and the cultural geography of Chobhar. Local explanations and ritual meanings should be confirmed with temple caretakers or residents when visiting.

How to experience it

Combine the temple with Chobhar Gorge and nearby heritage stops. The approach includes a climb, so allow time and wear comfortable shoes. Respect worshippers, ask before photographing ceremonies, and check local conditions and access before setting out.

BEFORE YOU GO

Travel details, access, opening arrangements, fees and local conditions can change. Confirm directly with local authorities or site caretakers before travelling. This guide is an editorial starting point, not a live access or booking service.

A question travellers ask

What is unusual about Adinath Temple?

Visitors often notice the metal pots, plates and utensils attached to the temple, which are associated with devotional offerings.
