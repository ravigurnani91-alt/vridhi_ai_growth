DISCOVER · CRICKET · INDIA

A 15-year-old hit 28 sixes. Now Indian cricket has a different problem.

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi hit 204 from 53 balls for India A. The records are extraordinary. The pressure that follows a 15-year-old may be the bigger story.

VRIDHI DISCOVER · 25 NOVEMBER 2026 · 6 MIN READ · THE STORY BEHIND THE RECORD

Fifty-two balls. That is how long Vaibhav Sooryavanshi needed to turn a cricket match into a record book.

Not 52 overs. Not 52 minutes. Fifty-two deliveries to reach 200 in a List A match — a format in which teams are normally given 300 balls each. He was 15.

By the time the teenager from Bihar was done in Doha on 24 November, the scoreboard said 204 from 53 balls. Twenty-eight of those deliveries had disappeared over the boundary. India A had chased down 250 against the UAE in just 14.2 overs, winning by seven wickets.

Read that again. A target that could have occupied an entire afternoon was gone before the match had properly settled.

The record was falling faster than anyone could update the graphic

His hundred arrived in 28 balls, beating the previous List A mark of 29, held by Australia's Jake Fraser-McGurk. The 150 came in 40. The double century arrived in 52 — less than half the previous fastest List A double hundred, which had taken 103 balls.

And then there were the sixes. Twenty-eight. No batter had hit that many in a single List A innings. Seven fours, 28 sixes: the boundary count tells you what kind of afternoon it was.

It is tempting to call this a T20 innings accidentally played in a one-day match. But even that undersells it. The target was 250. Sooryavanshi made 204 of them himself, at a strike rate of 384.90.

Here is the twist: he had been struggling just days earlier

Sport loves a clean superhero story. A prodigy arrives, destroys the opposition, breaks records and marches into the sunset. The real version is more interesting.

Before this innings, Sooryavanshi had made 26 against Nepal and 11 against Pakistan Shaheens. India A had lost to Pakistan A. The UAE match mattered: India A needed the win to secure a place in the semi-finals.

So the teenager walked into a must-win game carrying two modest scores — and walked out with a double century that had never been made this quickly in List A cricket.

There was a human moment in between. India Today's report says he spoke to his idol Yuvraj Singh for around 30 minutes during that difficult patch. He also leaned on his childhood coach, Manish Ojha. The reported advice was not some secret formula for hitting sixes. It was to settle down, read the conditions and trust his game.

“After facing a ball or two, I felt the bowlers weren't getting much help from the surface, so I backed my strengths.”

That is Sooryavanshi explaining his approach after the innings, as reported by India Today. Notice the order: he watched first. Then he attacked. The shots made the headlines; the judgement behind them may be what coaches want to study.

From Bihar to the biggest stage — at a speed of his own

Sooryavanshi came through Bihar's age-group and domestic cricket system. The country first began looking at him differently after his 35-ball IPL century in 2025. That innings made him a viral name. This one has made him a record-holder in List A cricket.

Reuters reported that he made his T20 international debut for India in July 2026, becoming the youngest Indian men's international debutant. His career is moving at a pace most players never experience. At 15, he is already being discussed in the same breath as records that senior professionals spend entire careers chasing.

That is extraordinary. It is also where the story becomes delicate.

India has a habit of turning young talent into national property

The moment a teenager does something unbelievable, the questions arrive. Is he the next great opener? When will he play for the senior side? Can he score 300? Is he the future of Indian cricket?

Some of those questions are natural. Some are premature. A record-breaking afternoon does not tell us how a young player will handle a long season, injuries, form slumps, unfamiliar conditions or the pressure of being expected to rescue every match.

And there is a cricketing caveat worth keeping in view. This was a remarkable List A performance, but it came against the UAE in an India A match. The opposition, conditions and match situation matter when judging what it means for the highest level. Acknowledging that does not take one run away from the 204. It simply keeps the analysis honest.

One innings cannot prove that one-day cricket has changed forever. It can, however, make every coach, selector and young batter ask a new question: what happens when a player can bring the scoring range of modern T20 cricket into a 50-over game — and sustain it long enough to make a double hundred?

The next test is not another 200

It is easy to demand another miracle. It is harder — and probably wiser — to let a 15-year-old become a better cricketer one match at a time.

Let him have a quiet game without calling it a crisis. Let him have a brilliant game without declaring his entire future settled. Give him coaching, fitness, competitive cricket and the freedom to learn. The best thing Indian cricket can do with a rare talent is not rush to spend it.

On 24 November, Vaibhav Sooryavanshi hit 204 from 53 balls. That is the headline. The story worth following is whether he can keep growing after the noise fades.

Vridhi's take: Celebrate the audacity. Study the skill. Keep the expectations human. A 15-year-old does not owe a country a legend's career because he gave it one unforgettable innings.
