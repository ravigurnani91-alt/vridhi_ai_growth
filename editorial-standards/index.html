OUR PROMISE TO READERS

Curiosity is free.
Evidence is essential.

Vridhi publishes explainers, reported briefs, essays and discovery-led stories. These standards explain what readers should expect—and what we will not pretend to know.

LAST REVIEWED · 21 NOVEMBER 2026

How we build a story

01 / THE QUESTION

A specific angle

We begin with a clear question, tension or real-world consequence—not a broad topic padded into an article. A headline should promise what the story actually delivers.

02 / THE EVIDENCE

Sources readers can inspect

We prioritise primary documents, official datasets, research papers, court or parliamentary records and direct institutional sources. Reputable independent reporting helps add context and scrutiny.

03 / THE EXPLANATION

Context, not filler

We explain why a fact matters, what it does not prove, who is affected and what remains uncertain. We avoid repeating generic background simply to increase word count.

04 / THE CHECK

Attribution and review

Names, dates, figures and quotations are checked against the linked source. Claims are attributed to the institution or person making them. Allegations are not presented as established facts.

What our labels mean

Verified fact

Supported by a source that can be inspected. Where a fact depends on an official statement, we say so rather than implying independent confirmation.

Analysis

Our interpretation of evidence, trends or likely consequences. Analysis is not a new fact and should be recognisable as interpretation.

Folklore & belief

Stories, deities, hauntings and traditions are treated respectfully as cultural material. We distinguish documented history, oral tradition, religious belief and unsupported supernatural claims.

Developing information

Early reports may change. We identify the date and source, avoid premature conclusions and update a story when material new evidence appears.

AI, ORIGINALITY & ACCOUNTABILITY

Tools can assist. They cannot take responsibility.

Digital tools, including AI-assisted research and drafting, may support discovery, organisation, editing and accessibility. They are not treated as sources of truth. Material claims must be checked against accessible evidence, quotations must be accurate, and generated text is not published as a substitute for reporting or original analysis. We do not knowingly publish fabricated citations, invented interviews, synthetic eyewitness accounts or unverified AI-generated images as documentary evidence.

Corrections are part of publishing.

If a material error is found, we aim to correct it promptly and make the nature of the correction clear. Updated articles should carry a revised date when the change is substantive. Readers should be able to identify the sources behind factual claims and report errors through Vridhi's contact channel.

OUR QUALITY TEST

Would this still be worth reading without the headline?

A Vridhi story should add something: original reporting, a useful synthesis of evidence, a clear explanation, a local lens, a practical tool or a genuinely fresh connection. We would rather publish less than fill the site with thin summaries, copied material or automated pages with no reader value.

These standards apply across Discover, Earth, Journal, Originals and Experiences. They are reviewed as the publication grows.
